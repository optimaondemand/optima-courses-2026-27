Course description

This course studies the human body by asking two questions of every structure: what is it? and what does it do? Form follows function, and each module ties the shape of a structure to the work it performs. Semester 1 begins with how the body is organized, then builds the body system by system: tissues and the skin, the skeleton, muscle, the nervous system, the endocrine system and the cardiovascular system. Systems are never treated in isolation; each lesson points out the connections to the systems around it.

Optima Academy Online teaches in the classical liberal arts tradition and is virtue-centered. Each module carries a virtue thread that runs through its lessons (listed below). The course also asks you to build what you study: in most lessons you draw and label the part of the body you are studying, so that by the end of a module you have assembled a figure of it yourself.

How each week works

Each full week of a module is built the same way, so you always know what to do next:

  • Four readings. Each reading introduces a topic in plain language, with figures, history-of-science notes and contemporary research where they fit.
  • Four lessons, one paired with each reading. A lesson gives you 2–4 activities and, in most weeks, a drawing session. Plan roughly 75–95 minutes per lesson.
  • Four assignments, one paired with each lesson, each with a rubric you can read before you start.
  • One reading quiz at the end of the week: multiple choice, true/false and matching, auto-graded.

Each module also has one writing assignment (an essay that pairs a structure with its function) and one Assembled Figure (a module-end submission of the figure you drew and assembled, with a short recorded caption; paper is highly recommended, and a digital drawing is also accepted). Modules 1 and 2 are single-week modules, so their writing assignments and Assembled Figures are shorter.

What you need

  • Access to Canvas and a reliable way to open the reading, lesson and assignment pages.
  • Paper (highly recommended) or a digital drawing app, pencils or colored pencils, and a phone or scanner to photograph your drawings.
  • A way to record yourself speaking (a phone or a recording app). Several activities ask you to talk through an idea aloud, in your own words and from memory, rather than write it.
  • A notebook and pen or pencil for sketches, tables and notes.

Totals count every graded item in the Semester 1 course shell except one optional Module 2 assignment (Cancer: When Cell Division Breaks Down, 8 points); your teacher decides whether it counts toward your grade. How the categories are weighted, and the grading scale, are set by your teacher and school.

Reading quizzes and the final exam are graded automatically. Assignments are graded by your teacher against the rubric printed at the bottom of each assignment page. Read the rubric first; it tells you exactly what a strong submission contains.

Finals week

Finals Week has three parts, taken in this order: the Review Lesson (a tour of all seven modules, no new content), the Review Activity (a six-system comparative chart, 24 points) and the Semester 1 Comprehensive Final (50 questions drawn from the readings of every module, one attempt).

Policies

Late-work, communication and grading-scale policies are set by your teacher and school, and your teacher will share them with you. If you have a technical problem photographing, scanning or uploading your drawings, contact your teacher before the due date.
